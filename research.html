Long term projects

LEVANTE - The Learning Variability Network Exchange

Dr. Michael C. Frank with supervision from Dr. Amy A. Lightbody

The Learning Variability Network Exchange is a project aiming to create a golden standard for measuring child development across cultures and countries. To do this, research institutions in different countries assess children using the exact same tasks with translations focused on cross-cultural relevance. As a research coordinator on the team, I helped organize extending these tasks for younger age groups (children under the age of 5), worked with translators and programmers on the task development, and interfaced with teams in different countries. Read more about the project through this blog post.

Generative AI’s impacts on Children’s Creativity Transfer

Dr. Guilherme Lichand

For my honors thesis in Education, I explored the impacts of using AI on two divergent thinking tasks for middle school students in Brazil. Through an analysis of over 500 students’ data, we find that students who use AI on one creativity task will perform worse on another creativity task compared with students who never had access to AI in the first place. We claim that this result can be attributed to AI’s negative impacts on children’s academic self-concept. You can read my honors thesis about an analysis of a smaller sample of 126 students here. An extension of this project is also discussed in the article “Reading ability conflates SES creativity gaps” in npj Science of Learning.

Race and Language

Dr. Ellen Markman with supervision from Dr. Marianna Y. Zhang

As an undergraduate research assistant for two years, I contributed to this project about how kids respond to common things parents say about racial disparities. I assessed over 90 kids, contributed to data analysis and final writing of a manuscript, and helped influence the experimental design of the forced choice questions in the study. You can read more about this project in the paper “How Colorblind and Structural Messages Affect Children's Reasoning About Novel Group Disparities” in Developmental Science.

Spring and Summer Long Internships

Social Skills Training with Large Language Models

Dr. Diyi Yang with supervision from Caleb Ziems

During the summer of 2024, I helped develop the frontend UI for a platform designed to teach people social skills through chatting with AI models. In this role, I also designed psychological surveys and conducted a small user study. I presented some of these findings in a poster session.

The Animal Number Town - Teaching Math to Children with Dyscalculia

Dr. Venod Menon with supervision from Dr. Hyesang Chan and Dr. Yunji Park

During the summer of 2023, I helped design games for children with Dyscalculia using FlutterFlow. I presented these findings in a poster session.

Dr. Jelena Obradović with supervision from Dr. Michael J. Sulik
